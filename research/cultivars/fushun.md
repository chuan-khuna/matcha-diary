# Fushun

Fushun is a high-yielding sencha cultivar from the national station at Kanaya, registered in 1991
as 茶農林41号. Its selling point is volume: NARO's handbook puts it thirty to fifty per cent above
Yabukita across both the first and second flush, and rates it particularly well on the cool
ground and poor soils where Yabukita underperforms. The name says as much — 富春, an abundant
spring, chosen with the stated wish that the cultivar would enrich the tea business.

Its more interesting role in this collection is as a parent.

## Lineage

- **Z1** ♀ — a numbered breeding accession held at Kanaya, never released as a cultivar.
- **Kanayamidori** ♂ (かなやみどり) — Cha Norin No. 30, itself a cross of the landrace selection
  S6 with Yabukita.

Forward, Fushun is the seed parent of **Seimei**, crossed with Saemidori. That matters for the
shape of the pedigree rather than for either cultivar's cup: Saemidori is Yabukita × Asatsuyu,
and Fushun reaches Yabukita through Kanayamidori, so Seimei descends from Yabukita along two
independent paths. It is the clearest case in this collection of why a pedigree has to be drawn
as a graph — a tree layout would have to either duplicate Yabukita or drop one of the two routes,
and the fact that the two routes exist is the whole point.

## History

The cross was made at Kanaya in 1965 and carried the trial designation F1-NN-71, renamed 金谷12号
in 1983 for the regional adaptability trials that ran from 1984 to 1990 across several
prefectures. MAFF registered it as 茶農林41号 in 1991; a separate Plant Variety Protection Act
registration, No. 3697, followed in October 1993. As with Fukumidori and Hokumei, sources quoting
one date or the other are describing two different registrations of the same plant.

## Characteristics

The bush is upright (直立型) with vigour above Yabukita's, which is enough of a habit that NARO
advises pruning young plants low to force branching. Plucking runs one to three days after
Yabukita — slightly late rather than genuinely late, and well short of the five-day figure some
English sources give (see `conflicts`).

Disease resistance is mixed and worth reading carefully, because it is reported the wrong way
round in circulation. Ring spot and bacterial shoot blight are medium or better; anthracnose and
mulberry scale are rated weak, and NARO's cultivation notes say plainly that both need a spray
programme where they are established. The claim that Fushun surpasses Yabukita on anthracnose does
not come from the breeder.
