# Akane

Akane is a black-tea cultivar, which in this collection makes it one of a small minority — the
others being Benihomare, Benifuki, Benihikari and Inzatsu 131 — and a survivor of a policy that
did not last. Between the wars and for a decade after, Japan tried to build an export black-tea
industry, and its breeding stations crossed introduced Assam material against local landrace
stock to get plants that would make a red tea and still survive a Japanese winter. Akane is one of
those, crossed in 1935 at the Kagoshima station and registered in 1953 in the same first Cha Norin
batch as Yabukita.

The industry it was bred for is gone. Japanese black tea is a niche product made by a handful of
growers, and the cultivars from that programme are mostly of historical interest — except
Benifuki, which found a second life as a source of methylated catechins for allergy research.

## Lineage

- **Ai21** ♀ — an Assam (*Camellia sinensis* var. *assamica*) introduction, アッサム21号.
- **NkaO3** ♂ — 鹿緑原3号, a Kagoshima zairai (landrace) selection.

The 茶農林 summary table garbles the female code; see `conflicts`. What is clear is the
shape of the cross: introduced Assam germplasm on the seed side for the black-tea chemistry,
Japanese landrace on the pollen side for hardiness. That is the same recipe as Benifuki two
generations later, and the deliberate opposite of Benihomare, which is simply an open-pollinated
seedling of introduced Indian seed.

## History

Crossed in 1935, selected out in 1939, and trialled from 1942 to 1952 under the strain name
鹿アッサム交配132号 — "Kagoshima Assam cross No. 132" — a designation that records exactly what
the programme was doing. Registration followed in 1953 as 茶農林15号.

The yield figures that survive are quoted against its stablemates rather than against Yabukita,
which is the giveaway that it was never meant to compete with a green-tea cultivar: about eight
per cent below Hatsumomiji and thirty-eight per cent above Benihomare.

## Characteristics

Mid-season. The bush is medium-sized and vigorous once established, but it is a late-rooting
cultivar and wants careful handling in propagation — a real drawback for a plant that has to be
multiplied by cuttings. Cold resistance is reported high, which was the whole point of crossing
Assam material against Kagoshima landrace stock rather than planting the Assam material directly.

The tea is a bright red liquor with a strong flavour and a crisp finish, attributed to high tannin
and low anthocyanin content, and the tannin roughly doubles in the second and third crops. The
name follows from that colour: あかね is madder red.
