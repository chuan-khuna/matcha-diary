# Hokumei

Hokumei is a Saitama sencha cultivar bred for the coldest ground in Japan that still grows tea,
and named for the ambition rather than the achievement: NARO's handbook records that the name was
chosen carrying the wish that the cultivar would spread at 茶栽培の北限 — the northern limit of
tea cultivation. Saitama's breeding programme had been aimed at that problem since the 1940s, and
Hokumei is the generation after Fukumidori: the same station, the same target, a cross made
against Sayamamidori instead of Yabukita.

## Lineage

- **Sayamamidori** ♀ — Cha Norin No. 5, itself a Uji zairai seedling selected in Saitama.
- **Saitama 13** ♂ — recorded by NARO's handbook under that station number, and by MAFF's registry
  as 5507, an open-pollinated Yabukita seedling. See `conflicts`; the two are plausibly the same
  line under two numbering schemes.

Whichever reading is right, Yabukita is in the ancestry on the pollen side, and Sayamamidori
carries Uji landrace stock on the seed side. Sayamamidori's other documented offspring —
Okumusashi and Toyoka — are therefore Hokumei's half-siblings, all three out of the same
prefectural programme.

## History

Registered in 1992 as 茶農林43号 under the station strain name 埼玉31号, and separately under the
Plant Variety Protection Act in November 1995 as registration No. 4775. Both dates appear in
circulation as "the" registration year; they are two different registrations of the same plant,
the same two-track pattern that makes Fukumidori appear as 1986 or 1988 depending on the source.

## Characteristics

Plucking runs three to five days after Yabukita, placing Hokumei on the late side of mid-season —
later than Yabukita, well ahead of Okumidori. The bush is upright (直立型) with vigour slightly
above Yabukita's, and yield is higher than Yabukita across both the first and second flush,
which NARO rates as on a par with Sayamakaori.

The cold-tolerance claim in the name holds up: against both winter cold-scorch syndromes, 青枯れ
and 赤枯れ, it rates stronger than Yabukita. Its disease package does not improve on its
grandparent at all — anthracnose, ring spot and mulberry scale are all rated weak, level with
Yabukita — so what the cultivar buys a northern grower is winter survival and yield, not a
lighter spray programme.
