# Takane-wase

Takane-wase is a sencha cultivar selected from open-pollinated Yabukita seedlings and registered
under Japan's Plant Variety Protection Act (種苗法) in 1985. Unlike the controlled crosses produced by
national and prefectural research stations, it was bred by a private grower, Muramatsu Hoichi,
working at Sakabe in Haibara-cho, Haibara-gun, Shizuoka Prefecture — a town later absorbed into
Makinohara City, on the Makinohara Plateau, one of Japan's largest single tea-growing districts and
the area credited with pioneering deep-steamed (fukamushi) sencha. Its defining trait, recorded in
its own official variety description, is speed: both its budding period and its picking period are
rated early, and both are named among the points that distinguish it from Yabukita. The same
document also compares it against three other early Shizuoka selections — Surugawase, Ōiwase and
Yamakai — but on shoot number, red-blight resistance and leaf characters rather than on earliness.
It belongs to the same loose cohort of privately registered wase (早生, early-budding) cultivars as
Satō-wase, Sagara-wase and Terakawa-wase: growers' individual selections registered under the Plant
Variety Protection Act rather than products of an institutional breeding programme.

## Lineage

- **Yabukita** — the seed (mother) parent. Takane-wase was grown from open-pollinated Yabukita seed, not
  a hand-pollinated cross.

Because the seed was collected from open pollination rather than a controlled crossing, Takane-wase's
pollen parent was never recorded and cannot now be identified — whatever pollinated the mother Yabukita
bush could have been any neighbouring plant. This is a narrower kind of unknown than a landrace selection
such as Yabukita's own zairai origin: there the entire parentage is anonymous, whereas here the mother is
a specific, well-documented cultivar and only the father is missing. No source found documents any
cultivar bred forward from Takane-wase in turn, and it does not appear in any of the master cultivar
registries or pedigree charts consulted for this record.

## History

The record here is thin and consists almost entirely of the registration itself. Muramatsu Hoichi, a
grower in Haibara-cho, Shizuoka, selected an individual seedling grown from Yabukita seed for its early
budding and picking. An application was filed on 20 February 1984 (application no. 1071), and the variety
was registered on 18 July 1985 under the Plant Variety Protection Act as Registration No. 898 — not a
MAFF Cha Norin (茶農林) number. The registrant's address on file, Sakabe 4401, Haibara-cho, Haibara-gun,
Shizuoka (postal code 421-0412), is also given as the breeder's own address: this was a grower's private
selection, filed and registered by the same person who found the plant, in the same pattern as Satō-wase,
Sagara-wase and Terakawa-wase. No source gives an earlier date for when the parent plant was actually
noticed or first propagated, so a selection year distinct from the 1984 application cannot be stated with
confidence.

The registry's own record of the breeder's rights is internally inconsistent in one respect worth noting
plainly: it lists an 18-year statutory term for breeder's rights, but also records the rights as having
lapsed (育成者権の消滅日) on 19 July 1994 — nine years after registration, not eighteen. No source found
explains the discrepancy; it may reflect an early termination (for example, non-payment of the annual
maintenance fee) rather than the full statutory term running its course, but that is not stated in the
record and should not be assumed.

Haibara-cho merged with the neighbouring town of Sagara-cho in 2005 to form Makinohara City — the
same area that later produced Sagara-wase — and the whole district sits on the Makinohara Plateau,
developed into tea land in the Meiji era and now first in Japan for crude-tea (aracha) output and
second only to Shizuoka City nationally in tea-garden area, known in particular for pioneering
deep-steamed sencha processing.

No dedicated release paper for Takane-wase was found in 茶業研究報告 (Tea Research Journal) on J-STAGE, nor
any record in CiNii Research or the National Diet Library catalogue, which is consistent with its origin
as a private grower's registration rather than a station-bred cultivar with a published trial programme.
It appears by name only, without further detail, in the two general cultivar-registry tables consulted
(Japanese Tea Sommelier's master list and My Japanese Green Tea's cultivar list), both of which confirm
the 1985 registration date and Yabukita-seed origin but add nothing beyond it. No current vendor, tea
shop or single-cultivar product bearing the Takane-wase name was found in this research, in contrast to
some of its wase cohort (Terakawa-wase, for instance, still turns up in small Mie Prefecture lots).

## Characteristics

Takane-wase is documented as early on both counts that matter for a wase cultivar: its own
registration text states that its budding period (萌芽期) and its picking period (摘採期) are both rated
early, and lists both as grounds for distinguishing it from Yabukita. Against the other three
comparison cultivars the registration rests its distinctness on other characters, so nothing in the
record places it ahead of those three. No source found quantifies that gap in days, so "early" here
should be read as officially confirmed rather than precisely measured.

The registration's plant description is otherwise detailed. Tree form is open (spreading) with strong
vigour and a somewhat large stool spread; trunk diameter and leaf-layer thickness are both medium. At
picking time the new shoots are somewhat large, numerous, somewhat long, and somewhat uniform in
development. Young leaves are long-elliptical, medium in size and thickness, green, with abundant fine
pubescence; mature leaves are long-elliptical, somewhat large, dark green, with somewhat glossy surfaces,
little leaf-margin waviness, and little curling. Branching is somewhat frequent with somewhat long
internodes. Cutting propagation is rated medium for rooting success. Yield potential is described as high
in young trees and somewhat high in mature trees, relative to the comparison cultivars, though no kg/10a
figures were recorded.

The finished product is described in the registration as superior in shape, with colour, aroma and
flavour all rated medium-to-upper, superior liquor colour, and high total nitrogen and amino acid content
— the last two typically correlating with a fuller, more umami-forward cup and lower relative
astringency, though no independent tasting note was found to confirm this in practice. Disease resistance
is reported as strong against both red blight (赤枯病) and bacterial wilt (青枯病); no data on cold
hardiness was found.

Set against its documented traits, Takane-wase reads as a competent, disease-resistant early selection
from good breeding stock, in a district that already produces some of Japan's most heavily processed
deep-steamed sencha. What is missing is any evidence that it went anywhere commercially: unlike Yabukita's
other early-budding relatives that spread into named regional specialities, no source in this research
found Takane-wase still in cultivation, in a shop, or in a tasting note. Whether that reflects the
cultivar quietly falling out of use after its Plant Variety Protection Act term lapsed in 1994, or simply
a private grower's selection that was never taken up beyond its own field, cannot be determined from the
sources available.
