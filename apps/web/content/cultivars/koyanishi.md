# Koyanishi

Koyanishi is a Shizuoka sencha cultivar registered in 1953 as Cha Norin No. 8 (茶農林8号), one of
the fifteen tea varieties in Japan's first-ever official cultivar registration — the same batch
that gave Yabukita its Cha Norin No. 6. It was selected by Sugiyama Hikosaburo, the same grower
who found Yabukita, from an individual tree that stood, by his own account, on the west side of a
hut on his test ground. Unlike Yabukita, Koyanishi never left Shizuoka: the 1953 record recommends
it for Shizuoka Prefecture only, and it was struck from the prefecture's own recommended-cultivar
list in 1955 — the very year Shizuoka began actively promoting Yabukita. Where Yabukita became the
coordinate system every other cultivar in Japan is measured against, Koyanishi is closer to a
footnote in the same breeder's catalogue: formally registered, briefly regional, then eclipsed.

## Lineage

- **An individual tree selected by Sugiyama Hikosaburo** — found on the west side of a hut
  ("koya", 小屋) on a planting in Shizuoka Prefecture; contributes the whole of the cultivar, as
  an unmixed landrace selection rather than a cross.

Koyanishi is a single-tree selection, not a controlled cross, so it has no recorded pollen parent
— the same situation as Yabukita. It was registered alongside two other Sugiyama selections in the
same 1953 batch, Yabukita (Cha Norin 6) and Rokuro (Cha Norin 9), with Makinohara-wase (Cha Norin
7, a separate Shizuoka selection by a different breeder) sitting between them in the numbering. No
source consulted for this record names a descendant bred from Koyanishi.

## History

### Registration, 1953

In fiscal Showa 28 (1953), the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry named its first-ever batch of
officially registered new crop varieties, and fifteen of them were tea — the same event that
produced Yabukita's registration. The record was published in 茶業研究報告 (Chagyo Kenkyu Hokoku,
the Tea Research Journal) No. 2, October 1953, under the title "茶の品種登録と命名" ("Registered
Tea Varieties and Their Naming"). The article notes that most of the fifteen had already spread to
some degree in cultivation before being formally registered, so the registration was catching up
with existing practice rather than launching something new.

Koyanishi's entry in that record gives: registration number Cha Norin No. 8; former strain name
(旧系統名) 小屋西; selected by Sugiyama Hikosaburo and developed between 1935 and 1946 (Showa
10–21) at the Shizuoka Prefectural Tea Experiment Station's Ministry-designated original-seed
orchard. Sugiyama — the same grower who, in 1908, picked Yabukita and Yabuminami from either side
of a bamboo grove — here named a tree for a different landmark on the same test ground: it stood
on the west side of a hut, giving "koya" (hut) plus "nishi" (west).

The 1953 record classes Koyanishi as chuban (medium-season), though picking is noted to begin a
little early for that class, and reports it as good quality for ordinary sencha (普通煎茶) — a
workmanlike rather than standout appraisal, and one limited explicitly to Shizuoka Prefecture: no
other suitable region is listed, unlike the national reach Yabukita would go on to have.

### A prefectural cultivar that lost to its stablemate

Japanese Wikipedia's biographical entry for Sugiyama records a detail the wider tea-cultivar
literature omits: Koyanishi, and Rokuro alongside it, had already been designated a Shizuoka
Prefecture recommended cultivar (奨励品種) in 1937 — sixteen years before its formal MAFF
registration. Both were then removed from that same prefectural recommended list in 1955. That is
the exact year Shizuoka began actively promoting Yabukita for planting. The timing reads as a
changeover rather than a coincidence: Koyanishi held prefectural favour through the interwar years
and into the immediate postwar period, and lost it in the same year, to the same breeder's other,
more versatile selection.

No source found documents Koyanishi in cultivation after that point. It does not appear in any of
the vendor, retailer or specialist reference sites surveyed for this record, nor on Ito En's
cultivar-characteristics page, nor in Kyoto Prefecture's own list of Uji-origin cultivars, nor
anywhere in the JSTAGE Tea Research Journal archive beyond the 1953 registration notice itself.

## Characteristics

The 1953 registration record is the only detailed characteristics source found for Koyanishi, and
it is brief. It is classed as a medium-season (chuban) cultivar, though picking is noted to begin a
little earlier than that class implies; no day-count relative to Yabukita has been sourced. Growth
habit is upright (直立) with strong vigour (樹勢強); the leaves are elliptical and large; the young
buds are described as somewhat yellow-green. Quality is reported as good for ordinary sencha (普通
煎茶として品質良好) — solid rather than distinguished, and the only appraisal recorded for it is a
plain "good," with none of the specific flavour or aroma notes given for cultivars from the same
batch that went on to wider release.

No yield figures, disease-resistance data, or cold-hardiness assessment survive in the sources
checked, and none is invented here. Nor does any cup description — aroma, flavour, colour — appear
in any source available: Koyanishi does not seem to have been written up as a tea to drink, only
registered as a plant. That absence is itself informative. Yabukita's profile of cold hardiness,
high yield and processing versatility is documented in detail precisely because those traits
carried it across the whole country; Koyanishi's characteristics were apparently sufficient for a
Shizuoka-only recommendation and no more, and once the same registry board had a better version of
the same basic proposition on offer in 1955, Koyanishi had no distinguishing trait on record to
fall back on.
