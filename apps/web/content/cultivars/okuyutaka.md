# Okuyutaka

Okuyutaka is the late version of Yutakamidori, and its name says so: おく for a late cultivar,
ゆたか taken straight from its seed parent. Registered in 1983 as 茶農林34号, it buds about eight
days after Yabukita and is plucked about six days after, which puts it near Okumidori in the
season and gives a grower planting both Yutakamidori and Okuyutaka a picking window stretched at
both ends.

## Lineage

- **Yutakamidori** ♀ (ゆたかみどり) — itself an Asatsuyu open-pollinated seedling.
- **F1NN8** ♂ (茶本F1NN8) — an unreleased Kanaya breeding line, recorded by MAFF's registry as
  たまみどり×S6.

This is one of the very few records in the collection whose grandparents are all documented here.
Tamamidori is a Uji zairai selection registered in the same 1953 batch as Yabukita; S6 is the
Shizuoka landrace selection that also produced Kanayamidori; and Yutakamidori descends from
Asatsuyu, another of the 1953 Uji selections. F1NN8 is also the pollen parent of Shunmei, so
Okuyutaka and Shunmei are half-siblings.

## History

The cross was made in October 1958 at the national station at Kanaya. Individual seedlings were
selected between 1964 and 1966, and the line carried the strain name 金谷3号 through clonal
comparison and regional adaptability trials that ran to 1982. MAFF registered it in May 1983.

Documentation is thinner than for its neighbours in the registry. NARO's current tea cultivar
handbook covers recent nationally funded releases and has no entry for a 1983 cultivar, so the
characteristics below rest on secondary compilation rather than the breeder's own publication.

## Characteristics

The headline is yield: around fifty per cent above Yabukita overall, and approaching double at
some trial sites and in some seasons. That, plus the late season, is the whole commercial case for
it — a late cultivar that also out-yields the reference is a straightforwardly good thing to plant
at the tail of a picking programme.

Field cold damage is reported lower than Yabukita's and anthracnose incidence low, which is what
put it in the recommended list for mountain districts. It is grown from southern Shizuoka and Gifu
southwards — Mie, Kyoto, Kochi, Kumamoto — rather than in the north, despite the cold tolerance.

In the cup it is mild and umami-heavy, with amino acid levels well above Yabukita's and slightly
lower catechins, which is the same profile its seed parent is valued for. Late budding and a high
amino-acid, low-catechin leaf is the combination that makes a cultivar worth shading, and it is
mildly surprising that Okuyutaka is not more often used that way.
