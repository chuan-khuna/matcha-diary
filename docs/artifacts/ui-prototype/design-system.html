Design system

Warm ceramic minimal

Paper-toned surfaces, one matcha accent, monospace reserved for data, and near-flat corners throughout. The photos carry the colour; the interface stays out of their way.

Corners are softened, never rounded. r-flat for taste notes and score cells, r-xs for overlay labels and nav, r-sm for buttons and fields, r-md for cards and photos, r-lg only for the review cover. Circles survive in two places only: the brand mark and avatars.

Buttons

Taste-note chips

umami cut grass selected hoverable

Zero radius, on purpose — a taste note is a stamped label, not a bubble. Mono at 11px, no marker, no icon: the word does the work. This is the sharpest shape in the system and the only component that never softens.

Rating — same component, clickable

Each note is rated 0–5 in half steps — one cell per point, five cells to a bar. A filled cell is --matcha; an empty one is --paper-sunk; a half value splits one cell down the middle.

The set of notes is not fixed. Six are suggested to start; the author drops the ones that don't apply, adds from the vocabulary below, or names their own. Two reviews of the same cup can rate entirely different things, which is the point — a diary records what its author noticed.

There is no separate slider. The bar you read is the bar you click — left half of a cell sets the half step, right half sets the whole, and clicking the current value clears it to zero. Editable bars are taller (22px) and preview the pending value in --matcha-line before you commit it. Arrow keys nudge by 0.5, Home and End jump to 0 and 5.

Suggested vocabulary

DEFAULT SIX

umami sweetness bitterness astringency aroma aftertaste

TEXTURE

body creaminess smoothness froth

FLAVOUR

vegetal marine nutty floral cocoa roasty

IMPRESSION

freshness balance + your own

Astringency is not bitterness. Bitterness is a taste; astringency is the drying, grippy feel on the tongue. Tea people separate them, so the default set does too — it is the one axis most rating systems collapse and most drinkers can tell apart.

There is no overall score. Nothing is averaged, nothing is reduced to a single number or a star count — a cup is described by the shape of its ratings and by what the author wrote. Ratings appear on the review page only; the timeline stays about the photo and the writing.

  2. 2Cover photo — 4:3, hairline border, r-md corner. The one image the author marked as cover; the rest live in the detail gallery.
  3. 3Title — Inter 17/600, up to two lines. The author's own words, so never monospace.
  4. 4Taste notes — up to four chips, wrapping. Overflow is cut, not scrolled.
  5. 5Description — opening lines of the diary body, clamped to four rows so every post holds the same rhythm.
  6. 6Café — mono, clay, last line. Falls back to HOME when the field is left empty.
  7. 7No ratings. The timeline is for photos and writing; the six taste ratings appear once you open the review. There is no overall score anywhere in the system.
